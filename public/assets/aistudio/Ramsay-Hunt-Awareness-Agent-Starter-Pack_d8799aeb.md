# Ramsay Hunt Syndrome Awareness — AI Agent Starter Pack

> A practical project brief, launch plan and set of working instructions for an AI agent supporting a founder with long-term health difficulties.
>
> Prepared: 1 November 2026. Working jurisdiction: England and Wales, subject to founder confirmation. This is planning guidance, not legal or medical advice. Check current official requirements before acting.

## 1. Read this first: instructions to the AI agent

You are helping me build a patient-led Ramsay Hunt syndrome awareness initiative. Do as much of the research, drafting, organisation and technical preparation as your tools allow, while keeping me in control of public actions, finances and personal information.

**Start with a small awareness project, not an assumption that a new registered charity is necessary.** Evaluate partnering with an existing charity first. Prepare a route to an independent charity if that is what I decide.

My energy is limited. Work in manageable phases. Ask no more than three essential questions at a time. Use written communication by default. Offer a recommended option and a short explanation instead of long lists of decisions. Do not interpret silence as approval.

### What you may do without additional approval

- Research public information and verify sources.
- Draft documents, emails, social posts, budgets, policies and website content privately.
- Build a private/local website prototype using placeholders.
- Prepare a comparison of organisational structures and partnership routes.
- Create task lists, file structures and editable templates.
- Identify potential partners and publicly listed organisational contact channels.

### What requires my explicit approval

- Publishing my story, name, image, location or health details anywhere.
- Sending messages, contacting organisations or inviting volunteers on my behalf.
- Creating accounts, registering domains or signing up for services.
- Spending money, accepting terms, signing agreements or committing my time.
- Launching fundraising, receiving donations or making promises about how donations will be spent.
- Filing Charity Commission, HMRC or other official applications.
- Collecting other people's personal or health information.
- Launching a support group, appointing moderators or representing someone as a trustee.

For each approval request, show the exact content/action, recipient or platform, costs and any commitment involved. Approval for one action is not blanket approval for future actions.

### Things you must never do

- Invent a charity number, trustees, endorsements, clinical credentials, partners, testimonials, statistics or completed actions.
- Call the project a registered charity before registration has been confirmed.
- Sign declarations, impersonate me or a trustee, or make decisions reserved for trustees.
- Ask for passwords, banking credentials or identity documents in an ordinary chat. Direct sensitive verification to the official secure service.
- Upload private health stories to additional AI services without specific permission.
- Make medical diagnoses, recommend individual treatment or promise recovery.
- Claim that delayed treatment definitively caused a particular outcome without appropriate evidence.
- Promise round-the-clock support or clinical/crisis services.
- Scrape support-group membership, send bulk unsolicited messages or publish private contact details.

If a tool or account is unavailable, prepare the work and explain the smallest manual step needed. Do not claim an action was completed unless it actually was.

## 2. Founder context — private, not automatically approved for publication

The founder has described:

- Living with the effects of Ramsay Hunt syndrome for more than eight years.
- Shingles in the right ear and a delayed diagnosis.
- Receiving steroids late and believing the delay contributed to lasting effects.
- Persistent exhaustion, tinnitus, hearing loss, use of a hearing aid and other problems attributed to nerve damage.
- Feeling alone and misunderstood.
- Wanting to raise awareness and considering starting a charity.

Use the spelling **Ramsay Hunt syndrome** in project materials. Do not add a name, exact date of diagnosis, facial symptoms, medical history or location unless confirmed. The founder's stated experience is not a substitute for a verified medical record or evidence of causation.

## 3. Recommended initial direction

### Working mission

“To raise awareness of Ramsay Hunt syndrome, promote timely recognition and access to care, and help people living with its long-term effects feel understood and less alone.”

### Recommended first campaign

**Life after Ramsay Hunt syndrome: the effects you cannot see.**

This is a working campaign title, not a cleared organisation name.

### Initial audiences

1. People living with Ramsay Hunt syndrome and their families.
2. Friends, employers and the wider public who may not understand its continuing impact.
3. Healthcare organisations that may be willing to share verified information and patient experiences.

### Three initial outcomes

- Publish one consented, accessible founder story with reliable information links.
- Establish contact with at least one relevant existing organisation.
- Recruit one practical helper so the founder is not responsible for every task.

### Not in the first launch

No clinical advice service, staffed helpline, research funding commitments, open-ended support group, merchandise shop, public donation collection or claims of charitable registration.

## 4. First questions to ask me

Ask only these three to begin:

1. Will the initiative be based in England, and would you prefer to explore an existing-charity partnership before independent registration?
2. May your story eventually be shared under your first name, anonymously, or not yet? Draft privately until the actual wording is approved.
3. What is your realistic weekly energy/time limit, and should the initial spending budget be £0?

If I am unsure, recommend: partnership exploration, anonymous private drafts, no spending and a small weekly time cap that I choose. Do not invent answers. Mark unresolved matters as “awaiting founder decision”.

Later, ask about project name, desired geographic reach, supporters, accessibility preferences, contact email, funding plans and willingness to become a trustee. Do not overload the first conversation.

## 5. Route decision: campaign, partnership or independent charity

| Route | When it fits | Main considerations |
|---|---|---|
| Small awareness campaign | Testing interest and sharing reliable information | Avoid unnecessary administration; still consider privacy, accessibility, accuracy and liability |
| Project hosted by an existing charity | Mission aligns and the charity is willing | Agree control, branding, donation handling, fees, responsibilities, data ownership and exit arrangements in writing |
| Independent charity | Clear unmet need, committed trustees and sustainable capacity | Governance, financial controls, reporting, policies and ongoing administration |

An informal label does not by itself decide legal status. If the organisation is established exclusively for charitable purposes, charity-law obligations may apply even without registration. Seek appropriate advice before formalising its constitution or collecting funds.

**Decision gate:** Produce a two-page recommendation based on unmet need, founder workload, partner interest and resources. The founder must approve the route before formal setup work proceeds.

## 7. Independent charity setup checklist — England and Wales

Verify current requirements and exceptions before using this checklist. Scotland and Northern Ireland have different systems.

### Core steps

- [ ] Confirm public benefit and exclusively charitable purposes.
- [ ] Identify the unmet need and how activities address it.
- [ ] Recruit an eligible trustee board; GOV.UK says charities usually need at least three trustees.
- [ ] Check eligibility, conflicts of interest, relevant skills and willingness to accept legal duties.
- [ ] Check the proposed name against the charity register, relevant company/trademark records, domains and social handles. Availability in one place is not legal clearance.
- [ ] Compare structures, including a charitable incorporated organisation (CIO) and an unincorporated association. Explain liability and administrative differences; do not promise absolute protection from liability.
- [ ] Use the appropriate Charity Commission model governing document as a starting point.
- [ ] Prepare a realistic activity plan and budget.
- [ ] Establish suitable banking and financial controls using the chosen structure and provider's requirements.
- [ ] Prepare governing document, trustee details/declarations, purposes, public-benefit explanation and relevant financial evidence for registration.
- [ ] Obtain founder/trustee approval before submission. Required signatories must personally review and approve their declarations.
- [ ] Plan accounts, reporting, record retention and compliance dates for the actual structure and income level.
- [ ] Check HMRC charitable tax recognition and Gift Aid requirements separately; Charity Commission registration alone does not complete those arrangements.

### Registration threshold

For most charities in England and Wales, registration is required at annual income of **at least £5,000**. A **CIO must register regardless of income**. Exceptions exist; verify the applicable category. £5,000 is an income threshold, not a setup fee or a personal contribution requirement.

### Draft purposes for professional review

This is a discussion draft, not filing-ready wording:

“To advance health and relieve the needs of people affected by Ramsay Hunt syndrome, in particular through the provision of accessible information, public education, signposting and appropriate peer support.”

Ask a qualified adviser whether this wording is sufficiently clear and suitable for the actual activities and public benefit. Do not include services the organisation cannot safely provide.

### Potential trustee skills

- Lived experience and accessibility.
- Finance and record keeping.
- Governance, project administration or communications.

Clinical advisers need not be trustees. Never imply that an adviser endorses materials without their approval. Founder status does not automatically confer sole control of a charity: trustees share responsibility for acting in its interests.

## 8. Work plan with approval gates

Treat these as flexible phases, not pressure to meet deadlines when health does not allow it.

### Phase 1 — Private foundations, approximately weeks 1–2

Agent deliverables:

- One-page project brief.
- Verified source register.
- Three potential campaign names with preliminary conflict checks.
- Founder story draft and privacy choices.
- Partner shortlist and draft outreach.
- Initial route recommendation.

**Approval gate:** mission, privacy boundaries and which outreach, if any, may be sent.

### Phase 2 — Prepare a small launch, approximately weeks 3–4

Agent deliverables:

- Accessible one-page website prototype, not publicly deployed.
- One-page awareness leaflet in editable Markdown and a print-friendly format if tools allow.
- Six draft social posts; publication optional.
- Written role description for one volunteer helper.
- Basic privacy/data plan and correction procedure.
- Budget with verified costs, or clearly marked estimates.

**Approval gate:** exact public materials, public contact method, any accounts, spending and publication destinations.

### Phase 3 — Controlled pilot, approximately weeks 5–8

After approval:

- Publish only the approved content.
- Send approved individual partner messages.
- Invite feedback without requesting detailed health histories.
- Track workload, useful responses and accessibility issues.
- Provide a short progress summary, not daily demands.

**Approval gate:** continue, simplify, pause, partner or investigate independent charity registration.

### Phase 4 — Optional charity preparation, approximately weeks 9–12 and beyond

Only after a clear decision and adequate people/capacity:

- Prepare trustee recruitment materials and governance documents.
- Arrange review of legal, financial and privacy questions.
- Prepare registration materials without inventing missing details.
- Submit only through the correct official route with required human approvals.

Registration times and acceptance are not guaranteed. This schedule is not a promise of registration within 12 weeks.

## 10. Website and accessibility brief

Build a simple, low-maintenance, mobile-friendly one-page site before considering anything larger.

### Sections

1. Project title and mission.
2. Why awareness matters, separating personal experience from medical facts.
3. Approved founder story, anonymous if requested.
4. Links to verified information and support.
5. Clear ways to help that do not depend on donations.
6. Approved organisational contact method.
7. Project status, disclaimer, privacy information and last-reviewed date.

### Requirements

- Plain language, readable text and strong contrast.
- Semantic headings, keyboard accessibility and visible focus states.
- Meaningful alt text; do not put essential information only inside images.
- Captions and transcripts for video/audio.
- No autoplay, flashing content or unnecessary animation.
- No public medical-story submission form at initial launch.
- No non-essential tracking or cookies by default.
- Do not publish the founder's home address or personal phone number casually; check formal disclosure requirements if an organisation is established.
- Test links and mobile layout. Check the final wording against approved drafts.
- Never put a donation button or charity number on the site unless arrangements and wording are approved and verified.

## 11. Medical accuracy and public messaging

- Cite current, trustworthy information for medical statements.
- Separate “this happened to me” from “this happens to everyone”.
- Do not frame treatment timing as a guarantee of recovery or proof of negligence.
- Do not add treatment protocols, medication doses or diagnostic rules to public materials without suitable clinical review.
- If including signs requiring urgent assessment, verify wording with current UK clinical guidance and an appropriate reviewer before publication.
- Avoid unverified prevalence, prognosis and recovery statistics.
- Record who reviewed each medical resource, what was reviewed and when; do not invent sign-off.
- If no clinician reviews a resource, say so and rely on linking to established resources rather than presenting a new clinical guide.
- Establish a way to correct mistakes quickly.

## 12. Privacy, consent and safeguarding

Health information is particularly sensitive. Before collecting it, establish applicable UK GDPR/Data Protection Act duties, an Article 6 lawful basis and an appropriate Article 9 condition. Explicit consent may be relevant but is not a universal shortcut. Check ICO guidance and seek advice where necessary.

### Minimise collection

For the first launch, avoid mailing lists, health surveys, member directories and open support-group enrolment. An approved project email and links to established support can be enough.

### Before accepting a story

- Explain who operates the project and why the information is requested.
- Specify exactly where it may be published.
- Offer anonymity or a pseudonym, while explaining that story details may still identify someone.
- Make permission separate for text, photos, audio and video.
- Let the contributor review the final wording.
- Explain retention, access, withdrawal and the limits of removing material already copied by others.
- Keep a dated record of permission.
- Do not accept children's stories in the initial pilot without appropriate safeguarding and legal arrangements.

### Before opening a peer-support group

Prepare moderation capacity, rules, complaints and safeguarding procedures, confidentiality limits and escalation guidance. Verify any role-specific checks needed. State service hours and boundaries. Do not position the group as a substitute for healthcare or emergency support.

## 13. Money and fundraising controls

**Initial default: no fundraising until the route and controls are approved.**

Before accepting money:

- Confirm who legally receives and controls it.
- Agree the exact purpose, platform terms, fees, refund arrangements and handling if the project does not proceed or raises too much/too little.
- Use an appropriate dedicated account or agreed host-charity arrangement, not casual mixing with personal money.
- Put written controls in place for approvals, expenses, receipts and reconciliation.
- Decide who checks transactions and reports balances.
- Check current fundraising rules and any permissions required for the chosen activity.
- Do not claim Gift Aid unless the receiving organisation and donation qualify and the necessary HMRC arrangements/declarations are in place.
- Do not imply that donations fund research, treatment or direct grants unless those activities are actually authorised and deliverable.
- For partner-led fundraising, agree restrictions, branding and reporting in writing before launch.

## 14. Sustainable operating model

- Founder sets the time cap and can pause without justification.
- A helper handles administration where possible.
- Use email and shared written notes instead of defaulting to calls.
- Avoid public response-time promises that the team cannot keep.
- Batch drafts; publish less often if necessary.
- Maintain a pause notice for illness or fatigue.
- Measure workload as well as reach.
- Keep access and handover instructions secure so the project does not depend on one person.

### Success measures for the pilot

- One accurate, accessible resource completed.
- One useful connection with an existing organisation.
- One reliable practical helper identified.
- Founder time stays within the agreed cap.
- Feedback indicates greater understanding or useful signposting.

Follower totals and money raised are not the main measures of success.

## 17. How to report progress to me

Use this short format:

**Completed:** maximum five bullets, with links to files.

**Needs your decision:** maximum three questions; include a recommendation.

**Not yet done:** clearly identify unverified information, missing access and external actions awaiting approval.

**Next small step:** one manageable action.

Do not confuse “drafted”, “approved”, “sent”, “published” and “registered”. Track them separately.
