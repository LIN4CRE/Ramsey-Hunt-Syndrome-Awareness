### Post 2: What is Ramsay Hunt Syndrome? (Verified Sourcing)
“Ramsay Hunt syndrome occurs when the varicella-zoster virus reactivates in the facial and auditory nerve pathways (Cranial Nerves VII & VIII).

Key early warning signs often include ear pain, a blister-like rash in or around the ear canal, and sudden facial weakness. Prompt antiviral treatment within 72 hours of symptom onset is vital.

If you or someone you know develops sudden facial weakness or severe ear pain with rash, seek urgent medical assessment immediately.

Verified clinical guidance via @FacialPalsyUK: https://www.facialpalsy.org.uk  
#HealthAwareness #EarlyRecognition”

### Post 3: Understanding Neurological Fatigue
“Why does a virus that affects facial nerves cause profound tiredness?

When cranial nerves sustain inflammation or damage, simple everyday tasks—listening through background noise, maintaining balance, focusing visually—require significant compensatory brain energy. 

Fatigue in Ramsay Hunt syndrome isn't laziness; it's the neurological cost of sensory compensation.

Be gentle with yourself, and share this with someone who needs to understand.  
#ChronicFatigue #SensoryOverload #RamsayHunt”

### Post 4: Living with Tinnitus & Hearing Changes
“Tinnitus is more than just a background sound—it can be an all-consuming ringing, buzzing, or roaring that interrupts concentration and sleep.

Many people living with Ramsay Hunt syndrome use hearing aids or sound therapy to manage long-term auditory nerve changes. 

Helpful evidence-based coping strategies and technology guides are freely available through @RNID and @TinnitusUK: https://rnid.org.uk  
#TinnitusAwareness #HearingLoss”

### Post 5: Ways to Support Without Spending Money
“You don't need money to make an immense difference for someone living with Ramsay Hunt syndrome:

1. **Be patient with listening:** Give people extra time in conversations, especially in noisy rooms.
2. **Respect pacing:** Understand when fatigue means plans need to be rescheduled without guilt.
3. **Share verified resources:** Help friends and colleagues recognize early symptoms promptly.
4. **Offer practical help:** Written messages rather than unexpected phone calls can make all the difference.

#KindnessMatters #SupportOthers”
