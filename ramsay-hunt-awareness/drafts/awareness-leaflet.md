## SIDE 1: Understanding Ramsay Hunt Syndrome

### Header: Life After Ramsay Hunt Syndrome: The Effects You Cannot See

#### What is Ramsay Hunt syndrome?
Ramsay Hunt syndrome is caused by the reactivation of the chickenpox virus (varicella-zoster) in facial and auditory nerve pathways (Cranial Nerves VII & VIII). It affects approximately 5 in 100,000 people annually.

#### Key Early Signs Requiring Prompt Medical Attention:
- Severe pain deep inside or around one ear.
- A painful, blistering rash in the ear canal, pinna, or on the roof of the mouth / tongue.
- Weakness or loss of movement on one side of the face (difficulty closing the eye, smiling, or speaking clearly).
- Dizziness, vertigo, changes in balance, or hearing loss.

**URGENT ACTION:** Early medical treatment with antiviral medication and corticosteroids within 72 hours of symptom onset provides the highest likelihood of full recovery. If you suspect Ramsay Hunt syndrome, contact your GP, NHS 111, or attend an Urgent Treatment Centre immediately.

## SIDE 2: The Long-Term Journey & Invisible Symptoms

#### Long-Term Challenges Many Face:
While visible facial asymmetry may improve over time, many individuals continue to live with ongoing invisible challenges:
- **Neurological Exhaustion:** Intense fatigue caused by the brain working harder to compensate for sensory nerve changes.
- **Tinnitus & Hearing Loss:** Persistent ringing or sound sensitivity (hyperacusis), often managed with hearing aids or white noise.
- **Synkinesis & Muscle Tightness:** Unintended muscle movements occurring when nerves regenerate.

#### Lived Perspective:
> *“What can be hardest is feeling that others cannot see the effort ordinary activities require. Underneath, managing sensory fatigue and hearing changes is an everyday reality. You are not alone, and your symptoms are valid.”*
